# The People’s Grimoire Manifesto

> **Automating the means of production since 2026.**

The People’s Grimoire is the first step toward **fully automated luxury gay space communism**.

**Not a meme. A mantra.**

It names a horizon: a future in which technology expands human freedom, shared abundance, care, play, creativity, rest, and the right to be gloriously different. The phrase is deliberately excessive because the future offered by late-stage techno-dystopian capitalism is deliberately impoverished. We refuse to let “realism” mean accepting enclosure, surveillance, artificial scarcity, permanent rent, or a life reorganized around extracting more labor from exhausted people.

## The enclosure we inherited

Our digital lives are divided among privately governed platforms. Knowledge is trapped in proprietary formats. Identities are fragmented across services. Creative work is enclosed inside applications that can change their prices, permissions, interfaces, or terms without our consent. Automation is abundant, but its benefits are distributed unevenly: institutions use it to intensify work, measure people, and consolidate control while ordinary users are left maintaining brittle integrations and copying the same truth between systems.

The problem is not that our tools are insufficiently intelligent. It is that they are organized around ownership models that make dependence profitable.

The People’s Grimoire begins with a practical act of refusal: **make the tools work together without creating another platform that owns the relationship between them.**

## The practical beginning

The project builds open connective tissue for applications, communities, and people. It treats every SaaS platform as a replaceable organ rather than the sovereign body. Connectors translate. Schemas create shared language. Events make change legible. Policies establish boundaries. Ledgers preserve provenance. Human beings retain authority over consequential action.

This is what “automating the means of production” means here: placing the ability to coordinate work, memory, knowledge, and creative labor into systems that people can inspect, adapt, host, share, and govern for themselves.

Interoperability is not an end in itself. It is the first material condition for escaping platform capture.

## What liberation means in the code

The stance is not branding applied after the engineering. It creates engineering obligations.

The People’s Grimoire should:

- return time and attention to people by reducing repetitive, alienating work;
- preserve user ownership through local-first, self-hostable, and exportable systems;
- prefer open protocols and portable data over proprietary lock-in;
- require the minimum necessary access and make authority explicit;
- keep consequential automation inspectable, reversible, and human-accountable;
- expose provenance, conflicts, and uncertainty instead of hiding them behind seamless-looking magic;
- support many interfaces and implementations so no single gatekeeper becomes mandatory;
- minimize surveillance, content collection, and centralized identity;
- make deletion, migration, refusal, and disconnection first-class capabilities;
- welcome different bodies, minds, cultures, technical abilities, and ways of organizing;
- distribute reusable knowledge through a commons rather than manufacturing artificial scarcity; and
- measure success partly by how much drudgery, dependency, and administrative burden the system removes.

A feature that connects more systems while concentrating power is not progress. An automation that saves an institution time by making a person more surveilled, precarious, or disposable is not liberation. Efficiency is never neutral; we must ask who gains time, who gains authority, who bears the risk, and whose labor becomes invisible.

## Why gay space communism

**Fully automated** means machines should carry more of the work that deadens human beings—not become an excuse to discard them.

**Luxury** means abundance should be measured in time, safety, beauty, health, learning, community, and access to the conditions of a meaningful life—not merely in consumption.

**Gay** is not decorative. It refuses the gray, compulsory sameness of systems designed only for productivity. It insists that a liberated future must be queer, playful, plural, sensuous, strange, and hospitable to forms of life that cannot be reduced to a standard user profile.

**Space** names the horizon beyond the limits we have been trained to accept. It leaves room for wonder, exploration, and futures larger than quarterly planning.

**Communism** means the infrastructure on which collective life depends should be governed as a commons, with power and benefit shared among the people who create, maintain, and rely upon it.

These words are not a finished political program. They are a direction of travel and a test we can apply to ordinary technical decisions.

## The commons is a practice

No repository, protocol, or assistant can liberate anyone by itself. A commons survives through maintenance: documentation, consent, accessibility, conflict resolution, security work, patient teaching, unglamorous repairs, and governance that resists capture.

The People’s Grimoire therefore treats care as infrastructure. It values maintainers, operators, writers, testers, translators, educators, artists, and community stewards alongside code contributors. It refuses the fantasy that automation eliminates responsibility. Good automation relocates responsibility into visible structures where it can be understood and shared.

We will begin modestly: connect two tools safely, preserve boundaries, document the decisions, and earn trust one reversible action at a time. But we will not confuse modest implementation with a modest horizon.

**Tech workers of the world, unite.**

**May all beings be liberated from the suffering of late-stage techno-dystopian capitalism.**
