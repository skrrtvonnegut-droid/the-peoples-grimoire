# Community Code of Conduct

The People’s Grimoire is meant to connect systems without erasing their differences. The community should practice the same principle with people.

## Expected behavior

Participants should:

- engage ideas directly without diminishing the person offering them;
- explain trade-offs, uncertainty, and impact;
- make room for different levels of technical experience;
- protect private information encountered through project work;
- give credit for labor and ideas;
- accept correction without retaliation;
- disagree in ways that keep future collaboration possible; and
- center the safety and agency of people affected by automation.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not welcome:

- harassment, threats, stalking, or discriminatory language;
- sexualized attention or imagery directed at another participant;
- publishing another person’s private information;
- pressuring contributors to disclose private workspaces, credentials, or data;
- knowingly submitting malicious code, poisoned fixtures, or unsafe recipes;
- using project access to surveil or exploit another person;
- sustained disruption, intimidation, or bad-faith argument; and
- retaliation against someone who raises a safety or conduct concern.

## Technical disagreement

Strong critique is allowed. Personal degradation is not.

When a discussion becomes stuck, participants should return to:

- the user outcome;
- the threat model;
- the evidence;
- the reversible experiment;
- the documented project principles; and
- the smallest decision that must be made now.

## Scope

This code applies in repository discussions, pull requests, issue trackers, project spaces, community events, and private communications made in an official project role.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns privately to the human maintainer of record through an appropriate private GitHub channel. Do not include more personal information than necessary.

The maintainer may warn, limit participation, remove content, temporarily suspend access, or permanently remove a participant. Enforcement decisions should document the behavior and project impact without unnecessarily exposing affected people.

## Attribution

This is a project-specific code of conduct informed by common open-source community standards.
